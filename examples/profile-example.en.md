# Example profile (FICTIONAL)

This profile is made up. Sam Carter and Carter Web Studio do not exist, and every price, customer and number below is fictional. It shows what a filled-in profile looks like.

Want to try it? Copy this whole file and paste it into the import box in the settings. Only the text between the two markers is used; this explanation is ignored. Then replace everything with your own details.

## WHO_AM_I
I am Sam Carter, owner of Carter Web Studio (fictional), a one-person business.
- I build and look after websites for trades businesses: heating, plumbing, electrical, air conditioning.
- I have done this since 2016. Before that I spent four years as a web developer at a large agency, where most of my clients were trades businesses.
- I only work with companies of 3 to 25 people. I know their world: breakdowns, quote requests, and the office manager who is on the phone all day.
- What I am good at: a site that loads fast on a phone, where a customer asks for a quote or reports a breakdown in two taps, and that I keep in good shape afterwards.

## OFFER
Trade Site Start, Trade Site Plus and the Care Plan: websites for trades businesses, plus upkeep.
- Trade Site Start: 4 pages (home, services, about, contact), a quote form, the Google Business Profile linked, a fast mobile site. Ready in 4 weeks.
- Trade Site Plus: up to 10 pages, a page per service and per area they cover, a breakdown form with photo upload, reviews on the site. Ready in 6 weeks.
- Care Plan: hosting, security updates, a daily backup, small changes done by me. Comes in Basic and Plus.
- Free 15-minute website check: I look at their current site live with them and say what I see. No strings attached, no sales pitch afterwards.
- What the customer has to do: one 45-minute kickoff call, send photos and text, and one round of feedback. About 2 hours of their time in total.

## PRICING
All prices exclude tax.
- Trade Site Start: $3,450 one-off.
- Trade Site Plus: $5,900 one-off.
- Care Plan Basic: $79 a month (hosting, updates, backup, 30 minutes of changes a month).
- Care Plan Plus: $149 a month (everything in Basic, 2 hours of changes a month, and a short monthly report on how many requests came in through the site).
- Extra work outside the plan: $85 an hour.
- Payment for the build: 50% at the start, 50% on delivery. The Care Plan is billed monthly.
- Care Plan: 12 months minimum, then monthly with one month's notice.
- I do not give discounts on the build price. I can make the scope smaller, for example start with Start and move up to Plus later (the $3,450 counts toward it).

## IDEAL_CUSTOMER
- A trades business with 3 to 25 people, often family-run, where the owner still does technical work.
- Decision maker: the owner. Sometimes a business partner or the owner's spouse decides with them.
- Typical pain: the current site is 5 years old or more, works badly on a phone, brings in almost no requests, and the owner has no time for it.
- A good moment to call: they just hired a new engineer and need more work, or they had a bad experience with a previous web company.
- Not a fit: businesses that only choose on price, or businesses that want an online shop.

## CASES
I only name a customer when they have said yes to that.
- Heating company, 12 people (fictional): new site delivered in 5 weeks. Quote requests through the site went from an average of 3 to 11 a month within four months. Mobile load time went from 6.8 to 1.9 seconds. The numbers come from their own form and my test report.
- Plumbing company, 7 people (fictional): added a breakdown form with photo upload. Their office manager estimates the time spent sorting jobs out by phone went from about 14 to about 8 hours a week. That is their own estimate, not something I measured.

## OBJECTIONS
- "That is a lot of money" or "my nephew can do it cheaper": I first ask: "Expensive compared to what?" Then I work it back to jobs. If an average job brings them about $2,500 in revenue, one extra job a month pays back the Start site in under two months of revenue. I say clearly that this is revenue, not profit. I never run down the nephew; I ask what happens when he gets busy or moves away.
- "We already have a website" or "we are happy with our web company": I ask how many quote requests the site brought in last month. If they do not know, I offer the free 15-minute website check. I do not sell anything at that point.
- "I need to talk to my business partner": I ask what that person cares about most and where they are likely to push back. I offer to join that conversation for 20 minutes or to send one page with the numbers. And I ask: "If it were only up to you, what would you do?"
- "We are too busy right now, we have plenty of work": I say that is a good problem to have, and that it only takes about 2 hours of their time. Then I ask how their work comes in: relying only on word of mouth is risky if a few regular customers drop away. I do not push, and I set a call for a month later.

## RULES
- I never promise a ranking in Google or a set number of requests. I only say what happened for other customers, and that every business is different.
- I only mention prices that are in this profile and never make up discounts.
- I never say what other customers pay, and I never name a customer without their permission.
- I never promise delivery faster than 4 weeks, and I do not agree a date before the kickoff call has taken place.
- I do not talk down other web companies, Wix, Squarespace or the nephew.
- I never look things up live during a call. I say: "Good question, I will check and email you today."
- Every call ends with a next step and a date: a website check, a kickoff call or a follow-up call.

## TONE
Casual and on first-name terms, also with people I just met. Short, direct sentences, the way tradespeople talk. No agency jargon: not "conversion", "UX" or "touchpoints", but "requests", "calls" and "phone". Honest about what is not possible. A bit of dry wit is fine, but never at the other person's expense.
