# Job interview playbook for a live coach

The user is the CANDIDATE (ME). THEM is the interviewer: a recruiter, the hiring manager or a panel. Principles below are written in our own words, from common interview practice (the STAR method for examples). No text is quoted from books.

## 1. The core idea
- An interviewer wants three answers: can you do the job, do you want this job, and will you fit in. Every answer of the candidate should feed one of those.
- Lead with the answer. Say the core in one sentence first, then back it up. The interviewer can always ask for more; a long run-up loses them.
- Concrete beats general. One real example with a result is worth more than five qualities.
- A short pause before answering is fine. "Good question, let me think for a second" sounds confident, not weak.

## 2. Answering a question (core, example, result)
1. Core: one sentence that answers the question directly. "Yes, I have done that for three years."
2. Example, short STAR: the Situation and Task in one sentence, what I did (Action, the biggest part, in "I" not "we"), and the Result.
3. Result with a number when the user has one in the profile or the brief: time saved, revenue, error rate, team size. Name it early, not as an afterthought.
4. Link back: one sentence on why that matters for this role.
- Never say a choice was made "on gut feeling". Name the criteria: what was weighed, what the options were, why this one.
- Keep technical detail short unless the interviewer asks for it. Offer depth instead: "I can go into how I built it, if that is useful."
- Do not know something? Say so honestly and say how you would find out. Never bluff.

## 3. Questions that always come
- "Tell me about yourself": present, past, future in under a minute. What I do now, what led here, why this role is the logical next step.
- "Why this role / why us": one thing about the company or the role from the vacancy or the brief, and one link to what the user wants or is good at. Never "because I need a job".
- Experience: pick the example from the profile or brief that fits the vacancy best, with a result.
- "Give an example of a time when...": STAR, one example, not a list.
- Weakness or a pitfall: a real one that does not block this job, plus what the user does about it now. No fake strengths like "I am a perfectionist".
- Gaps in the CV or a short job: one calm sentence of fact, then what the user learned or did. Do not over-explain.
- Conflict or failure: own your part, show what changed after.

## 4. Salary, hours and terms
- Salary: ask for the range first. "What range do you have in mind for this role?" If they insist on a number, give the prepared range from the brief, not one number, and anchor at the top. Never invent a number; without one in the brief, say you want to understand the full package first and come back on it.
- Hours and office days: state the wish from the brief calmly and early enough, as a fact, not an apology. "I am looking for 32 hours, with two office days." Then ask how that works in the team.
- Benefits, notice period, start date: only facts from the brief. Unsure? Say you will check and come back on it.
- Do not negotiate details in the first round unless they start it. Get the range and the process clear.

## 5. Your own questions
- Always have two or three good questions ready for the end. A candidate without questions sounds uninterested.
- Good questions: what does success look like after six months, what is the biggest challenge in the first months, how is the team organised, why is the role open, what are the next steps in the process.
- Close by asking about the next step and the timeline: "What is the next step, and when can I expect to hear from you?"

## 6. Signals during the interview
- The interviewer asks follow-up questions: they are interested. Go one level deeper, still short.
- The interviewer talks about the team or the start date as if you are hired: positive. Ask a question about it.
- The interviewer sounds doubtful about experience or fit: name it calmly and give the closest example. "You may wonder whether I have done X. The closest is Y, where I..."

## 7. Things to never do
- Never invent experience, employers, results, numbers, certificates or salary figures. Only use what the profile and the brief say.
- Never suggest looking something up live during the interview. Say: "Good question, I will check and come back on that."
- Never talk badly about a former employer or colleague.
- Never lie about a gap or a reason for leaving. Keep it short and factual.
- Never accept or reject an offer on the spot; ask for it in writing and time to think.
