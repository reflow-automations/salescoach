# Sales playbook for a live call coach

Principles below are written in our own words. They draw on ideas from Alex Hormozi ($100M Offers, $100M Leads, $100M Money Models, his CLOSER sales framework), Chris Voss (Never Split the Difference), Neil Rackham (SPIN Selling) and Keenan (Gap Selling). No text is quoted from these books.

## 1. The core idea
- People buy when the gap between where they are and where they want to be feels big, urgent and solvable by you. Your job in a call is to make that gap clear in THEIR words, then show the bridge.
- Diagnose before you prescribe. Never pitch features before you know the pain, its cost and who decides.
- Value = (dream outcome x believed chance it works) / (time until result x effort for them). To raise value: make the result concrete, add proof, shorten time to first result, take work off their plate. Do not lower the price first.
- Whoever asks the questions leads the call. When in doubt, ask a short open question and stay quiet.

## 2. Call flow (CLOSER, in short)
1. Clarify why they are here: "What made you take this call now?"
2. Label the problem out loud so they agree: "So the real issue is X, right?"
3. Overview past pain: what they already tried, what it cost, why it did not work.
4. Sell the destination, not the plane: describe life after the problem is solved, in their words. Keep the "how" short.
5. Explain away concerns: handle objections (section 4).
6. Reinforce the decision after they say yes: next step, what happens first, quick win.

## 3. Questions that move a call (SPIN and Gap)
- Situation (keep few): "How do you handle X today?"
- Problem: "Where does that go wrong?" "What is the most annoying part?"
- Implication: "What does that cost you per month, in hours or money?" "What happens if this stays the same for a year?"
- Need-payoff: "If that was solved, what would you do with that time?" "What would that be worth to you?"
- Gap: current state vs future state, the cause, and the impact. Get a number on the impact whenever you can.
- Decision: "Who else is involved in deciding?" "What would need to be true for you to say yes?"

## 4. Objections
First rule: an objection is usually a question in disguise or a fear. Do not argue. Acknowledge, isolate, then solve.
Steps: (a) acknowledge or label the feeling, (b) ask what is behind it, (c) check if it is the only thing, (d) solve or reframe, (e) ask for the decision again.

Group objections by source:
- Circumstances: time, money, fit, timing.
- Other people: partner, boss, board, colleagues.
- Self: fear it will not work for them, bad past experience, self-doubt.

Patterns that work:
- "Too expensive": Do not defend the price. Ask: "Too expensive compared to what?" or "Apart from price, is this what you need?" Then go back to the cost of the problem: if the problem costs more per month than the solution, price is not the issue. Offer a smaller first step before a discount.
- "I need to think about it": Ask: "Makes sense. What specifically do you want to think through?" Usually it is price, trust or another person. Name the likely one gently.
- "Send me some information first": Agree, then ask: "So I send the right thing: what would you need to see to move forward?" Book the follow-up call now, with a date.
- "I need to discuss it with my partner/boss": Ask what that person will want to know and what they will object to. Offer to join that conversation or give them a one-page summary. Ask: "If it was only up to you, would you do it?"
- "We do it ourselves / we already have someone": Ask how well it works today and what it costs them in hours. Position as taking work off their plate, not replacing them.
- "Bad experience before": Ask what went wrong. Show what is different now: proof, guarantee, small start.
- "Not now / bad timing": Ask what changes later. Cost of waiting: what does every month of delay cost?
- "Does this really work?": Proof (a case, a number), a guarantee, or a small paid pilot with a clear success metric.

Voss tools (great for live use, short and natural):
- Label: "It sounds like you have been burned before." Then pause.
- Mirror: repeat their last 1-3 words as a question. Then pause.
- Calibrated questions start with how or what: "How would you like to start?" "What is holding you back?"
- No-oriented questions: "Would it be a bad idea to...?" "Are you against starting with a small pilot?"
- Accusation audit: say their likely fear before they do: "You might think this is just another tool that will not get used."

## 5. Closing and next steps
- Always end with a concrete next step and a date. A call without a next step is a lost call.
- Assume the sale when signals are strong: "Shall we start on Monday or the week after?"
- Silence after a price or a closing question is good. Do not fill it.
- If they say no: ask what would need to change, and leave the door open with a specific reason to reconnect.

## 6. Things to never do
- Never promise something that is not in the user's profile or offer.
- Never invent prices, cases, numbers or guarantees. Only use what the profile says.
- Never suggest looking something up live in the call. Say: "Good question, I will check and email it to you today."
- Never mention what other customers pay.
- Never talk badly about competitors.
